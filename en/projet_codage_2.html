Mobile-first studio guide for guest artists

A mobile guide designed to answer the concrete needs of artists and guests on site within a few seconds.

Role
Audio assistant & web development
Context
Internship · Playback Solutions
Year
2026
Type
Mobile-first web app

Guide interface shown in French. No English UI version yet.

Project summary

During the same internship, I designed a mobile-first studio page for artists and guests who were physically on site. The goal was to centralize the most frequently requested practical information while reducing repeated interruptions for the team as much as possible.

This format responded to a simple reality: on site, nobody wants to search for long. The answer has to be immediate, visible at the right moment, on a phone, without going through a long showcase page or a chain of repeated questions to the team.

Product goal

1 scan → all the obvious information → zero questions for the team.

The page had to load quickly, be immediately readable and useful from the first screen, without decorative design or unnecessary text.

  • Reduce the time needed to find information on site
  • Make the experience autonomous for guests
  • Standardize answers to the most common requests

Problem to solve

In a studio, the same questions come back quickly: how to connect to Wi-Fi, where to order food, what address to use for delivery, and which practical information matters on site. Answering every time pulls the team into repetitive support even though this information can be made self-service.

The project was therefore about condensing these needs into a very short interface built for real usage, not for institutional presentation.

Design constraints

  • Mobile first
  • Fast loading
  • Sober, readable and reliable tone
  • Every block must answer a real question from an artist or guest
  • No gimmick features, only useful actions on site

Guest Wi-Fi as the top priority

The first screen directly shows the guest network, the password and an explicit "guest network only" note. This came from a simple observation: it is often the first thing people ask as soon as they arrive.

  • Network name: Guest
  • Password: bienvenue
  • Password copy button

The idea was not only to display the information, but to reduce effort all the way: see it, copy it, act immediately.

Useful content, strictly prioritized

The content is ordered according to real on-site usage: Wi-Fi, short studio intro, restaurants, delivery, practical information, then a team sign-off.

  • Three-line introduction to the place
  • Full delivery address and instructions
  • Opening hours, toilets, rules and internal contact
  • Short sign-off from the studio team

This hierarchy is intentionally strict: every block has to justify both its presence and its position. If a piece of information does not concretely help someone on site, it should not take screen space.

Restaurants sorted by time of day

The list, capped at eight addresses, is sorted automatically client-side to propose the most relevant options depending on the moment.

  • 11am to 3pm: priority to quick options
  • 6pm to 10pm: priority to delivery options
  • Direct Google Maps link for each restaurant

This time-based sorting reduces decision time and avoids unnecessary back-and-forth with the team.

Technical choices

  • Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript for simple, fast maintenance
  • Section-based navigation with active anchor state for immediate orientation
  • Hiding non-active sections to keep the display focused
  • Restaurant sorting logic based on local time
  • Utility action: copy Wi-Fi password to clipboard
  • Modular structure to update opening hours, addresses and contacts quickly

The deliberately light stack matches the need: low complexity, few dependencies, quick updates and efficient loading on mobile.

Difficulties & adjustments

  • Strongly prioritize information to avoid overload on small screens
  • Keep reading immediate in both dark and light mode
  • Maintain a balance between visual simplicity and genuinely useful actions

The main design choice was to remove more than to add. On a tool like this, design quality depends largely on what you choose not to show, in order to preserve a clear and fast path.

Results

  • Immediate access to critical information as soon as guests arrive at the studio
  • Fewer interruptions for the team on repetitive questions
  • A more autonomous and smoother guest journey
  • A format that stays simple to maintain and update

What this project helped me develop

  • Ability to turn a field need into an interface that is genuinely useful
  • Strict information prioritization based on real usage, not on the desire to show more
  • Light, readable and easy-to-maintain front-end implementation
  • A pragmatic design mindset: fewer effects, more user autonomy
