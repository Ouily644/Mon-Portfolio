Suivea redesign - logo & mobile interface

Redesign of the Suivea logo and mobile mockups to improve interface clarity, visual coherence and future front-end integration quality.

Role
Graphic design, mobile UI/UX, prototyping
Context
MMI second-year internship · Docaposte · Suivea
Period
May 2021 - July 2021
Type
Visual identity & mobile interface

Mockups shown in French. No English UI version yet.

Project summary

During my second-year MMI internship at Docaposte, I worked on Suivea, a tracking tool used for mail and parcel flows. The mission covered two complementary parts: redesigning the visual identity through the logo, and modernizing the mobile interface.

The goal was not to modernize for its own sake, but to improve a dense interface and an aging visual identity while keeping the landmarks needed by people already familiar with the product.

Product context

Suivea is used in a business environment with field usage across several devices: desktop, mobile, tablet and Android smartphones. The redesign had to stay clear, robust and aligned with the company’s internal guidelines.

  • Keep strong readability in fast-paced real-world contexts
  • Reinforce coherence between visual identity and product interface
  • Produce mockups that could be used directly for front-end integration

Initial diagnosis

The existing product worked functionally, but the whole system lacked unity. The interface felt more dated and denser than necessary, with inconsistencies in hierarchy, spacing and coherence from one screen to another.

The redesign therefore had to improve readability without breaking familiar usage patterns, which called for precision more than disruption.

Mission 1 - Logo redesign

I reworked the existing logo toward a direction that was cleaner, more legible and more stable at small sizes, while preserving the product’s existing visual cues: simple shapes, immediate readability and a blue color system. The aim was not to change the identity, but to make it more coherent and better aligned with the UI redesign.

  • Exploration of several directions to test balance, simplicity and readability
  • Checks on how the logo held up at small sizes and in different use cases
  • Preservation of useful brand landmarks to avoid an unnecessarily abrupt break
  • Final version selected after iterations and team feedback

Mission 2 - Mobile interface redesign

I then redesigned the Android mobile mockups, reworking visual hierarchy, spacing and consistency across screens. The main data-entry page served as a reference base to harmonize the rest of the user flow.

  • Redesign of navigation such as navbar and footbar to clarify priority actions
  • Reorganization of entry and distribution screens to reduce visual load
  • Standardization of components, icons, spacing and labels
  • Production of mockups that were more directly usable for implementation

Design criteria

  • Highlight main actions without overloading the screen
  • Improve quick reading in a field-work context
  • Create screens coherent enough to form a real system, not a series of isolated pages
  • Reach a level of precision compatible with faithful front-end integration

Working method

The project moved forward through short iterations with regular feedback from my internship supervisor and the product team. This feedback loop made it possible to adjust graphic choices quickly, reinforce consistency between screens and align the design with business needs.

Difficulties & tradeoffs

  • Finding the right balance between modernization and visual continuity
  • Maintaining strong readability on mobile screens that could become very dense
  • Making multiple stakeholders’ feedback converge without losing overall coherence

The main tradeoff was to simplify and clarify without designing against actual usage. In a business tool, a cleaner interface is only valuable if it remains immediately understandable for the people who rely on it every day.

Results

  • Final logo selected as the basis of Suivea’s project identity
  • Mobile mockups validated and usable for integration
  • A more coherent visual system for the next product iterations
  • A clearer base to move into the front-end implementation phase

What this project helped me develop

  • Ability to redesign an existing product without breaking useful landmarks
  • Attention to readability and hierarchy in a dense business context
  • Ability to produce mockups that already anticipate future implementation
  • Understanding of the relationship between visual identity, interface and real-world usage
