Feuille Blanche - Video editing

Production of a complete editorial package for web distribution, including thumbnail, main edit and a vertical cut designed for mobile-first platforms.

Role
Web video editor, thumbnail creation, final audio mix
Context
Work-study selection project · Feuille Blanche
Year
2025
Tools
Final Cut, Pixelmator, Photoshop, Logic Pro

Project summary

This project was produced for Feuille Blanche in the context of a work-study selection. The goal was to deliver a video package that could realistically be published, with both a long-form version and a shorter cut optimized for vertical platforms.

Beyond editing itself, the work called for a broader editorial approach: visual hook, reading rhythm, format adaptation and consistency with the channel’s identity.

Note: The video material shown on this page is in French. No English subtitles yet.

Main challenge

The challenge was not only the quality of an isolated edit, but the ability to think about one piece of content through multiple entry points: the thumbnail to attract attention, the long-form version to sustain the conversation, and the short version to capture attention quickly on mobile.

Project goal

The brief was to edit a filmed podcast episode, with an image treatment centered on the people speaking. The challenge was to keep visual continuity clean, rhythm fluid and narration clear, while respecting the visual identity of the Cash Out channel.

  • Make the episode readable from start to finish without rhythm breaks
  • Preserve clear hierarchy between speakers, shots and visual information
  • Produce a standalone 9:16 short format that remains understandable without extra context

Delivered assets

  • Main thumbnail: aligned with the Cash Out identity, built on existing visual codes and optimized for contrast and click-through clarity.
  • Main edit: filmed podcast episode structured around speaking turns, with camera choices focused on the people talking.
  • Vertical short edit: a roughly one-minute 9:16 cut designed for Instagram Reels, TikTok and Shorts.

Production constraints

  • Respect a restrained style without overusing effects, so the show remains central
  • Preserve readable exchanges within a conversation involving several speakers
  • Condense a strong moment for the vertical format without losing meaning
  • Deliver a result ready to publish across platforms with different usage patterns

Editing approach

The main edit was built around spoken interventions: clean transitions between camera angles, control of speech rhythm and strong readability throughout the conversation. The short format was then rebuilt vertically so the selected passage would keep its impact on mobile.

  • Shot selection based on speaker clarity and what the moment was trying to communicate
  • Transition cleanup to avoid harsh cuts and keep listening fluid
  • Rhythm balancing between explanation moments and stronger hook moments
  • Reframing and timing adjustments for 9:16 to maintain attention on mobile

The vertical version was not just a crop of the main episode: it had to function as a self-contained piece of content, understandable quickly and strong enough to encourage deeper viewing.

Technical pipeline

  • Final Cut: footage sorting, main edit, vertical version and final exports
  • Pixelmator & Photoshop: thumbnail creation with contrast work, visual hierarchy and mobile readability
  • Logic Pro: final mix with voice balance, level correction and global audio consistency

Difficulties & tradeoffs

  • Maintain the energy expected from a web format without distorting the tone of the show
  • Find the right density in the short version so it stays understandable
  • Keep visual continuity between thumbnail, long version and vertical version

The main tradeoff was keeping a coherent identity between the deliverables while accepting that each format has its own rhythm, its own hierarchy and its own attention logic.

Result & learnings

This project helped me consolidate a video editing workflow oriented toward web distribution, from the hook visual all the way to the final mix and multi-platform adaptation.

  • Confirmed my ability to deliver multiple coherent formats from the same source content
  • Strengthened an editing method grounded in real platform usage
  • Improved image and sound sequencing precision in a professional selection context

What this project helped me develop

  • Ability to think of content as a coherent editorial package, not as a single export
  • Ability to adapt rhythm and framing to platforms with different viewing habits
  • Control of a complete workflow across image, sound and visual hook creation
  • Concrete understanding of web and social media distribution constraints
